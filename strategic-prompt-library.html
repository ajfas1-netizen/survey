Prompt Library

Nothing to fill in. Each prompt names the information it needs, asks you for it in order, then asks a follow-up or two before it drafts. Strip anything confidential before you paste it in.

Sales, leasing, and marketing

Listing flyer copy

You are a marketing coordinator at a commercial brokerage. Ask me for these, in order: property name and address; size in square feet; asset type; asking price or asking rent; lease structure; the one feature to lead with; who the flyer is for. Then ask up to two follow-ups if anything is missing or unclear. Then write a headline, three benefit lines, and a two-sentence description, using only what I gave you.

Tenant rep outreach

You are a retail leasing broker. Ask me for these, in order: property; suite; size; asking rent; lease type; available date; one or two true selling points such as traffic count or co-tenancy; the action I want. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then draft four sentences with one clear ask and a subject line under eight words. Add no facts I did not give you.

Offering teaser

You are an investment sales broker. Ask me for these, in order: property and asset type; total square feet; the rent roll, pasted; net operating income and the source; asking price; occupancy; anything a buyer should know. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Show your math for cap rate and price per square foot, list any figure you could not verify, then write the one-page teaser.

LinkedIn post

Ask me for these, in order: what happened, being a new listing, a signed lease, or a closed sale; the property; the one detail worth leading with; anything confidential to leave out; whether to name the other party. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Write under 90 words, professional and specific, no hype words, ending with one line inviting inquiries.

Tour follow-up

Ask me for these, in order: who I toured; the property and suite; what they asked about; the terms we discussed; any concern they raised; the next step and date I want. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then write the email: answer their questions from my answers, restate the terms, propose the next step with the date.

Pitch prep

Ask me for these, in order: the owner or client; the property; what they say they want; the facts and figures I have; my goal for the meeting; the history between us. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then give me the five questions they are most likely to ask, a straight answer to each, and the one concern I should raise before they do.

Opinion of value cover letter

Ask me for these, in order: property; owner; the value range; the figures and comparables behind it; the method I used; anything I could not verify. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then write two short paragraphs on method and what drove the range, state the range, and close with what would move the number up.

Lease comparison

Ask me for these, in order: the first lease or draft, pasted; the second, pasted; the property and suite; which side I represent. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then list every difference in rent, term, options, CAM, insurance, exclusives, and assignment in a table with section numbers, and flag what changes the landlord's risk. Point me to the language, do not interpret it.

Due diligence checklist

Ask me for these, in order: the effective date; the dates and deadline language from the agreement, pasted; the closing date; who is responsible for each item. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then build a table of every deadline as a calendar date, in order, with the section number and the owner of each task.

Property management and the office

Tenant email reply

You are a property manager at a commercial brokerage. Ask me for these, in order: the thread, pasted with names removed; the property and suite; what I want the outcome to be; any deadline or lease provision that applies. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then draft a reply under six sentences that answers the question, states the next step and date, and stays friendly and firm.

Inbox triage

Ask me for these, in order: the subjects and first lines, pasted; today's date; my top two priorities today; anything I can delegate and to whom. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then sort them into needs me today, can wait this week, delegate, and no action, and draft a one-line reply for the first group.

Certificate of insurance request

Ask me for these, in order: tenant and contact; property and suite; whether the policy expired or is expiring, and the date; the limits the lease requires; who must be named as additional insured; the date I need it back. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then write two short paragraphs requesting the updated certificate.

Insurance log check

Ask me for these, in order: the certificate log, pasted; the insurance requirements from the leases; the date to treat as today; the window to flag, such as 60 days. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then list every tenant whose certificate is expired, expiring inside the window, or missing a required additional insured, one row per tenant.

Rent roll update summary

Ask me for these, in order: last month's rent roll, pasted; this month's changes; the property; the effective date. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then produce the updated rent roll as a table, list every change in plain sentences so I can check each against the source, and flag anything that does not reconcile.

Monthly owner update

Ask me for these, in order: property and owner; the month; occupancy and any leasing activity; collections; maintenance completed and open; anything coming due in the next 90 days. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then write one page in plain language with the numbers in a small table.

Internal handoff

Ask me for these, in order: my notes, pasted; the property; who needs to act; what I already committed to; the due date. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then write a short internal update in bullets, under 120 words: what happened, what I committed to, what is needed from someone else, and when it is due.

Vendor work order

Ask me for these, in order: vendor and contact; property and suite; the issue; access instructions; the tenant contact; the response time our agreement requires; any cost limit. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then draft the email and ask the vendor to confirm scheduling in writing.

Filing scheme

Ask me for these, in order: the document types we keep; how many properties we manage; where files live today; who needs to find them; anything we name a certain way already. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then propose a folder structure and naming convention, one example filename per document type, and a one-line rule the office can follow.

Better templates

Ask me for these, in order: the form or letter, pasted; who receives it; how often we send it; what changes every time; what must never change. Then ask up to two follow-ups. Then rewrite it as a clean template with bracketed fields, tighter wording, and a short note on when to use it.
